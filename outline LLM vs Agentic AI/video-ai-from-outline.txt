Video Outline: LLM vs. Agentic AI, and Why Context-Aware Training Matters Before that: change Hải and Hiếu to character A and B The video is an English explainer for a general technical audience, “Hải” and “Hiếu” are example characters (Hải chats, Hiếu gives a task).

1. Hook / core question Why isn’t a chatbot enough for real work? Why does training an LLM on data that understands context matter?
2. LLM vs. Agentic AI (Boss and Workers analogy) Category LLM (The Boss / CEO) AI Agents (The Workers) Role The reasoning engine that understands instructions, generates plans, and can coordinate tasks. Autonomous components that pursue assigned goals using reasoning, tools, and actions. Output Generates text, structured responses, decisions, and tool instructions. Produces results through actions, such as retrieving data, executing code, or generating responses. Knowledge Uses knowledge learned during training and information provided through context. Can use external retrieval systems. Accesses relevant information through memory, databases, APIs, documents, and other tools. Time Does not inherently have access to real-time information. Can retrieve up-to-date information through external tools and services. Planning Analyzes the overall goal, creates a plan, divides tasks, and coordinates agents (when acting as an orchestrator). Executes assigned tasks, makes local decisions, evaluates results, and reports back.

Key line: LLM = chatting, assign task. Agentic AI = getting it done. 3\. Example scenario Hải chats with the LLM, asks a question, and gets a text answer based on the past. Hiếu sends a request to the Boss. The Boss reads the message and breaks it down. The Boss instructs all workers (agents) to work together. The workers return a result that is deployed and runs immediately (for example, a working website). 4\. The conflict from the last meeting of Vietnam &amp; Korea Vietnam’s approach Approaches: Knowledge distillation from existing large language models. Generate synthetic training data using existing models and fine-tune smaller models. Transfer selected capabilities from teacher models to student models. Potential weaknesses: Capability dependency: The student often inherits the teacher's limitations and biases. It does not automatically acquire capabilities beyond the information and supervision available during training. Limited generalization: If training data is narrow or repetitive, the student may perform well on familiar tasks but struggle with unfamiliar problems. Knowledge loss: Some complex reasoning capabilities, knowledge diversity, and nuanced behaviors may not transfer completely. Model collapse risk: Indiscriminate recursive training on synthetic data can reduce data diversity and model quality.

Korea’s approach Respect data copyright and licensing. Develop proprietary local-language datasets. Build local benchmarks to measure language understanding and cultural awareness. Improve contextual understanding rather than focusing only on translation accuracy. 5\. Why a more advanced LLM is needed It must produce more correct results by learning context. A prompt can contain context, but natural-language instructions may be incomplete or ambiguous. The Boss must write better instructions for the workers (system instructions), so the actions are clear, correct and actionable. Example: the user speaks their own language, while the agent works internally in another language. The system needs context-accurate translation, not just word-for-word translation. 6\. Analogy: the translator Two people graduate from the same university, and a company must choose a translator: one lives in Korea, one in Vietnam. \=&gt; The one in Korea is the better choice because they understand the cultural and situational background. Lesson: Accurate translation requires understanding meaning, intent, and context, not just translating individual words. 7\. Conclusion / goal Build on high-quality real-world local data: Develop LLMs that understand local languages, cultural context, and domain-specific knowledge. Combine multiple data sources: Use licensed real-world data, existing multilingual models, and carefully validated synthetic data rather than relying entirely on one source. Develop local benchmarks: Evaluate whether models understand user intent, contextual meaning, and real-world requirements. Continuously improve the model: Use evaluation results and human feedback to identify weaknesses and improve performance. \=&gt; A smarter Boss leads to better-directed workers. By improving the LLM's contextual understanding and reasoning capabilities, AI agents can execute tasks more accurately and deliver results that better match users' actual intentions.
